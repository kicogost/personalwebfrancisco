I once had the CV I'd been told to want. Management consulting in London, the
right logos in the right order. On paper I'd made it. Then I woke up one night
soaked in sweat, convinced I was having a heart attack. It wasn't. It was my
body telling me something my calendar wouldn't.

I changed almost everything in the months that followed. Left what needed
leaving. Started stretching every morning, sitting still for ten minutes, and
getting choked by strangers three times a week. None of it was complicated. All
of it was daily. Within a few months I was living a different life, and I've
been building on that ground ever since.

Three things I took from it.

Hard work is still the only thing that works, and it has never been harder to
do. AI will get you to okay on anything, instantly. Your phone will pay you in
dopamine for doing nothing at all. Both are useful and both are quietly
expensive. Becoming someone who can still work hard, in an age engineered to
make that feel unnecessary, is a rare and compounding advantage. I optimise for
it.

Systems beat one-off outputs. A good week isn't a heroic week. It's a week
where the thing ran without me. Anyone can produce a result once. The work is
building the machine that produces it every time.

Real work is the content. I don't write about things I haven't done. Everything
I publish is downstream of something I shipped, broke, or paid for myself.

The rest is simpler than people make it. Be kind to yourself first, then your
body, then everyone else. Hold your ideals firmly and change them when you're
given a good reason. Fight when it's time to fight, forgive when it's time to
forgive. Say please, thank you, and I'm sorry.

Marcus Aurelius put it better than I can. Stop arguing about what a good man
should be. Be one.
